Two optional rules about what follows a stop, then the rules that govern the most frequent consonant change of all, m before a consonant.

## h after a stop

<SutraFolio id="8.4.62" level={2} />

After a stop (<Sa iast="jhay" />), *h* may take a form like the stop before it (*pūrvasavarṇa*, as in the last lesson). Varadarāja says which: *h* is voiced and breathy, so it becomes the **voiced aspirate** of that row, the fourth sound of the row — <Sa iast="vāg" /> + <Sa iast="hariḥ" /> → <Sa iast="vāgghariḥ" /> or <Sa iast="vāg hariḥ" />.

<Voice quote="kas-8.4.62" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā runs the rule through every row that can end a word (*vāgghasati*, *śvaliḍ ḍhasati*, *agniciddhasati*, *triṣṭubbhasati*): four, since by this stage no word ends in a palatal stop. Then it asks why the rule says "after a jhay". Because after a nasal nothing happens: *prāṅ hasati*, *bhavān hasati*.
</Voice>

## ś after a stop

<SutraFolio id="8.4.63" />

After a stop, *ś* may become *ch* when a vowel, h, y, v or r follows it (<Sa iast="aṭi" />). Varadarāja's example takes three rules in a row, in the order of their numbers:

A vārttika (<Sa iast="chatvam amīti vācyam" />) extends the change to the wider class <Sa iast="am" />, which adds l and the nasals: <Sa iast="tacchlokena" />, "by that verse".

<Voice quote="kas-8.4.63" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā's pairs show the option after a velar, a retroflex and a labial: *vāk chete* or *vāk śete*, *śvaliṭ chete* or *śvaliṭ śete*, *triṣṭup chete* or *triṣṭup śete*. Each pair uses *śete*, "lies down", so that the ś is followed by a vowel, as the rule requires.
</Voice>

## m becomes anusvāra

<SutraFolio id="8.3.23" />

A word ending in *m* takes anusvāra (ṃ) before any consonant: <Sa iast="harim" /> + <Sa iast="vande" /> → <Sa iast="hariṃ vande" />, "I praise Hari".

<Voice quote="kas-8.3.23" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā marks both limits of the rule. Before a vowel the m stays: *tvam atra*, *kim atra*. Inside a word it stays too: *gamyate*, *ramyate*, where the m is not word-final.
</Voice>

<SutraFolio id="8.3.24" />

Inside a word, n and m become anusvāra only before a stop, sibilant or h (<Sa iast="jhal" />): <Sa iast="yaśāṃsi" />, "glories", <Sa iast="ākraṃsyate" />, "he will step up". Before other consonants they stay: <Sa iast="manyate" />, "he thinks".

## The anusvāra takes the next sound's nasal

<SutraFolio id="8.4.58" />

Before a consonant other than a sibilant or h (<Sa iast="yay" />), an anusvāra becomes a sound of the following consonant's class: before a stop, its nasal. Inside a word this is compulsory: in <Sa iast="śāntaḥ" />, "calm", from the root *śam*, the m has become anusvāra before t (8.3.24) and the anusvāra has become n.

<SutraFolio id="8.4.59" />

At the end of a word it is optional: <Sa iast="tvaṅ karoṣi" /> or <Sa iast="tvaṃ karoṣi" />, "you do". Both spellings are correct, and printed texts use both.

<Voice quote="kas-8.4.59" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā writes one sentence twice to show the whole range: every word-final anusvāra before a stop either takes that stop's nasal or stays anusvāra. *taṅ kathañ citrapakṣaṇ ḍayamānan nabhaḥstham*, or *taṃ kathaṃ citrapakṣaṃ ḍayamānaṃ nabhaḥsthaṃ*: five nasals, one at each place.
</Voice>

## Words that keep their m

<SutraFolio id="8.3.25" />

In <Sa iast="samrāṭ" />, "emperor", the m of *sam* stays m before *rāj*.

<SutraFolio id="8.3.26" />

Before h followed by m, a word-final m may stay m: <Sa iast="kim hmalayati" /> or <Sa iast="kiṃ hmalayati" />. A vārttika adds: before h followed by y, v or l, the m may become a **nasalised** y, v or l — <Sa deva="किय्ँ ह्यः" /> or <Sa iast="kiṃ hyaḥ" />, "what of yesterday?".

<SutraFolio id="8.3.27" />

Before h followed by n, the m may become n: <Sa iast="kin hnute" /> or <Sa iast="kiṃ hnute" />.

<Voice quote="vp-1.106" voice="darsana">
  Faint or loud, says Bhartṛhari, the sphoṭa, the word as a unit, keeps one and the same measure; only the stream of sound that carries it swells and shrinks. In the same spirit, the optional rules of this lesson change how a junction sounds, not which words are said: *vāgghariḥ* and *vāg hariḥ* are the same two words.
</Voice>
