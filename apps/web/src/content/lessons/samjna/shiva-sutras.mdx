The Laghusiddhāntakaumudī opens, before its first numbered rule, with fourteen lines. Tradition calls them the **Māheśvara** or **Śiva sūtras**. Each line lists a few sounds and ends with one consonant that is not part of the list. The red letters below are those final consonants.

## What is in the lists

Read the fourteen lines in groups and a design appears:

1. **Lines 1–4 are vowels**: simple *a i u*, then *ṛ ḷ*, then the diphthongs *e o*, then *ai au*.
2. **Lines 5–6 are semivowels**, *ya va ra la*, and the aspirate *ha* is placed among them.
3. **Line 7 is the five nasals**: *ña ma ṅa ṇa na*.
4. **Lines 8–10 are voiced stops**: the breathy ones (*jha bha gha ḍha dha*) before the plain ones (*ja ba ga ḍa da*).
5. **Lines 11–12 are voiceless stops**: breathy first, then plain.
6. **Line 13 is the sibilants** *śa ṣa sa*, and **line 14** repeats *ha*.

Consonants are written with an *a* only so they can be pronounced. That *a* is not a member of the list. And long vowels do not appear at all. You will see why when you reach <SutraRef id="1.1.69" />, which lets a short vowel stand for its long forms.

## Why not the alphabet order?

The alphabet groups sounds by place: the five velars, then the five palatals, and so on. Pāṇini's rules more often need groups by **manner**: all voiced stops, all voiceless sounds, all nasals. The Śivasūtras are ordered so that each of those groups is one unbroken stretch.

- Every voiced unaspirated stop (*j b g ḍ d*) sits in line 10, so one short name covers all five.
- Every voiceless sound, from *kh* through *s*, is one stretch from line 11 to line 13.
- Every consonant is one stretch, from the *ha* of line 5 to the *l* that closes line 14.

## Two oddities, both deliberate

- ***ha* appears twice**, in line 5 and again in line 14. That lets it belong to two different groups: the voiced sounds that begin at line 5, and the sibilant-and-*h* group at the end.
- **The red ṇ closes two lines**, 1 and 6. A name ending in ṇ is therefore ambiguous, and Pāṇini resolves each case. Try it on the board: choose *a*, then the second red ṇ.

<Voice quote="vp-1.1" voice="darsana">
  Bhartṛhari's first verse calls the Word *akṣara*: the imperishable. The same word names a letter or syllable. The Śivasūtras are the Word laid out as its smallest audible parts, and the rest of the grammar builds those parts back up into language.
</Voice>

## The markers are tools
