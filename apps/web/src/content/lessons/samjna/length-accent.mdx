## Length by measure

<SutraFolio id="1.2.27" />

Pāṇini does not say "a short vowel is brief". He gives a measuring stick. Inside the sūtra, *ū* stands for three reference vowels at once: short *u*, long *ū*, and prolated *ū3*. Varadarāja's gloss unpacks it as <Sa iast="uś ca ūś ca ū3ś ca vaḥ" />. A vowel that lasts as long as the first is **short** (<Sa iast="hrasva" />, one *mātrā*), as long as the second is **long** (<Sa iast="dīrgha" />, two), and as long as the third is **prolated** (<Sa iast="pluta" />, three).

Prolated vowels are rare in ordinary prose. They appear, for instance, when someone is called from a distance, and they are written with a 3: *devadatta3*.

## Nasality

<SutraFolio id="1.1.8" />

A nasal vowel sounds through mouth and nose together. Any vowel can be nasal or oral, which doubles the count again.

Why count at all? Because the Śivasūtras list only *a*, not eighteen *a*'s. Something has to let one short letter stand for every variety, and that is the job of <SutraRef id="1.1.69" /> in the next lesson.

<Voice quote="vp-1.48" voice="darsana">
  Length is a matter of time: one, two or three measures. Bhartṛhari observes that sound is born in sequence while the word itself has no before and after; it only appears in the form of a sequence. The mātrās measured here belong to the sound that reveals the word, not to the word itself.
