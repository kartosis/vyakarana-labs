Before a vowel or a voiced consonant, a visarga does not stay a visarga. What it becomes depends on two things: the vowel before it, and whether it comes from *s* or from an original *r*.

## s becomes ru

<SutraFolio id="8.2.66" level={2} />

Every word-final *s* becomes <Sa iast="ru" />, the *r* marked with *u* that you met with *n* and *m* in the last module. Before a voiced sound nothing turns it into visarga, so it can be heard as *r*: <Sa iast="agnis" /> + <Sa iast="atra" /> → <Sa iast="agnir atra" />, "the fire is here" (the Kāśikā's example). The marker matters. The rules below name *ru*, so they apply to an *r* that replaced *s*, and not to a word that always ended in *r*, such as <Sa iast="punar" />, "again", or <Sa iast="prātar" />, "early".

## After short a, before short a

<SutraFolio id="6.1.113" />

After a short *a*, *ru* becomes *u* before a short *a*. Neither *a* may be prolonged (<Sa iast="pluta" />). The *u* then meets the *a* before it, and the vowel rules you learned in the second module take over:

There is a puzzle here. *ru* comes from 8.2.66, in the last three pādas, and those rules are invisible to the rules of chapter 6 (<SutraRef id="8.2.1" />). How can 6.1.113 see it? The Kāśikā's answer: a rule that is about *ru* depends on it, so for that rule *ru* is not invisible.

<Voice quote="kas-6.1.113" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā tests every word of the rule. Not after *i*: *agnir atra*. Not after long *ā*: *vṛkṣā atra*, which belongs to the next lesson. Not for an original *r*, which has no marker: *svar atra*, *prātar atra*. And before *i* the rule does not apply either: *vṛkṣa iha*.
</Voice>

## After short a, before a voiced consonant

<SutraFolio id="6.1.114" />

The same happens before a voiced consonant (<Sa iast="haś" />): *ru* after short *a* becomes *u*, and *a* + *u* become *o*. Varadarāja: <Sa iast="śivo vandyaḥ" />, "Śiva is to be praised". There is no avagraha this time, because no vowel follows.

<Voice quote="kas-6.1.114" voice="bhasya">
  *puruṣo yāti*, *puruṣo hasati*, *puruṣo dadāti*: "the man goes, laughs, gives". Before *y*, *h* or *d* alike, *-aḥ* becomes *-o*. This is the visarga sandhi you will meet most often: almost every word in *-aḥ* does it before a voiced consonant.
</Voice>

  To understand *śivo 'rcyaḥ* you take it apart again: *śivaḥ*, *arcyaḥ*. Bhartṛhari describes exactly this. A sentence is one whole, he holds, but the mind meets it first by grasping parts; his own commentary says the sounds set the mind working "in the form of grasping parts that appear as sounds and words".
