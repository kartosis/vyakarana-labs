A word that ends in *s* or *r* is written with a visarga when nothing follows it: <Sa iast="rāmaḥ" />, <Sa iast="punaḥ" />. That is its form at a pause. The Visarga-sandhi-prakaraṇa, LSK 103 to 114, asks what the *s* or *r* becomes when another word follows. This lesson takes the voiceless consonants; the next two take the vowels and voiced consonants.

## The path to s

Three rules from the last module do most of the work. A word-final *s* first becomes *ru* (<SutraRef id="8.2.66" />, next lesson). Before a voiceless consonant, *ru* or *r* becomes visarga:

<SutraFolio id="8.3.15" compact />

and before a voiceless consonant, visarga becomes *s*:

<SutraFolio id="8.3.34" compact />

That *s* then takes the place of the sound after it, by ścutva and ṣṭutva (<SutraRef id="8.4.40" />, <SutraRef id="8.4.41" />). Varadarāja's examples are in the first lesson of the last module:

<div class="example">
  <span><Sa iast="rāmaḥ" /> + <Sa iast="cinoti" /> → <Sa iast="rāmaś cinoti" />, "Rāma gathers"</span>
  <span><Sa iast="rāmaḥ" /> + <Sa iast="ṭīkate" /> → <Sa iast="rāmaṣ ṭīkate" />, "Rāma goes"</span>
  <span><Sa iast="vṛkṣaḥ" /> + <Sa iast="tarati" /> → <Sa iast="vṛkṣas tarati" /> (the Kāśikā's example on 8.3.34)</span>
</div>

## Before a sibilant: two forms

<SutraFolio id="8.3.36" level={2} />

Before *ś*, *ṣ* or *s* (<Sa iast="śar" />), 8.3.34 would give *s*, which ścutva or ṣṭutva turns into the same sibilant. This rule offers a second form: the visarga may simply stay. Varadarāja: <Sa iast="hariḥ śete" /> or <Sa iast="hariś śete" />, "Hari lies down".

<Voice quote="kas-8.3.36" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā gives one pair for each sibilant: *vṛkṣaḥ śete* or *vṛkṣaś śete*, *vṛkṣaḥ ṣaṇḍe* or *vṛkṣaṣ ṣaṇḍe*, *vṛkṣaḥ sāye* or *vṛkṣas sāye*. Then it adds a vārttika: when the sibilant is itself followed by a voiceless consonant, as in *sthātāraḥ*, the visarga may also be dropped. That gives three correct forms of *vṛkṣāḥ sthātāraḥ*, "trees that stand": *vṛkṣā sthātāraḥ*, *vṛkṣāḥ sthātāraḥ*, *vṛkṣās sthātāraḥ*.
</Voice>

## Before k and p

<SutraFolio id="8.3.37" compact />

Before a velar or labial stop, the visarga does not become *s*. It stays, or turns into one of two breath sounds made at the place of the next stop: the <Sa iast="jihvāmūlīya" />, at the root of the tongue, before *k* and *kh*, and the <Sa iast="upadhmānīya" />, at the lips, before *p* and *ph*. In Devanagari they are written <Sa deva="ᳵ" /> and <Sa deva="ᳶ" />. The Kāśikā's pairs: <Sa deva="वृक्षᳵ करोति" /> or <Sa iast="vṛkṣaḥ karoti" />, and <Sa deva="वृक्षᳶ फलति" /> or <Sa iast="vṛkṣaḥ phalati" />, "the tree bears fruit". Most printed texts write the visarga.

<Voice quote="kas-8.3.35" voice="bhasya">
  One more rule, which Varadarāja leaves out, covers a voiceless consonant that is followed by a sibilant, as in *kṣ*, *ps* and *ts*. There the visarga stays visarga: *puruṣaḥ kṣuram*. The Kāśikā notes why the rule says "visarga becomes visarga" instead of just "no change": so that even the breath sounds of 8.3.37 are ruled out.
</Voice>

<Voice quote="vp-1.72" voice="darsana">
  *hariḥ*, *hariś*, *rāmaṣ*, *vṛkṣas*: the end of the word changes with each sentence. Bhartṛhari holds that one word is found in different sentences, as a sound stays one in different words. His own commentary compares the variation to the reflections of one thing in shade, a mirror or water.
</Voice>
