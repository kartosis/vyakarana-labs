The last lesson left two cases open: *ru* after *ā*, and *ru* after *a* before any vowel but *a*. Both go through a *y* that you rarely hear.

## ru becomes y

<SutraFolio id="8.3.17" level={2} />

After *a* or *ā*, and after the three particles <Sa iast="bhos" />, <Sa iast="bhagos" /> and <Sa iast="aghos" /> (particles of address that end in *s*), *ru* becomes *y* before a vowel or a voiced consonant (<Sa iast="aś" />). Before a vowel, after *a* or *ā*, Śākalya's rule from the vowel-sandhi module may drop that *y* (<SutraRef id="8.3.19" />). Since 8.3.19 is invisible to the vowel rules of chapter 6 (<SutraRef id="8.2.1" />), the two vowels then stay apart. Varadarāja: <Sa iast="devā iha" /> or <Sa iast="devāy iha" />, "the gods are here". After the particles, which end in *o*, another rule that Varadarāja leaves out drops the *y* every time (<SutraRef id="8.3.20" />): <Sa iast="bho atra" />; the Kāśikā allows only a lightly pronounced *y* in its place (<SutraRef id="8.3.18" />).

<Voice quote="kas-8.3.17" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā's examples cover both conditions. After the particles: *bho atra*, *bhago dadāti*. After *ā*: *brāhmaṇā dadati*, *puruṣā dadati*, "the brahmins give, the men give". After short *a* before a vowel other than *a*, its example on this rule is *ka āste* or *kay āste*, "who sits?".
</Voice>

## Before a consonant the y always goes

<SutraFolio id="8.3.22" />

Before a consonant the *y* is dropped, and not only in Śākalya's view: in everyone's (<Sa iast="sarveṣām" />). Varadarāja: <Sa iast="bho devāḥ" />, "O gods!", <Sa iast="bhago namaste" />, "O blessed one, homage to you", <Sa iast="agho yāhi" />, "go, sinner". The same holds after *ā*: the Kāśikā's <Sa iast="vṛkṣā hasanti" />.

## ahan keeps an r

<SutraFolio id="8.2.69" />

The word <Sa iast="ahan" />, "day", replaces its *n* with *r* where no case ending follows it: in the nominative and accusative singular, whose ending has been deleted (repeated in <Sa iast="aharahaḥ" />, "day after day"), and as the first member of a compound (<Sa iast="ahargaṇaḥ" />, "a series of days"). This is an original *r*, not *ru*, so it does not turn into *o* the way *-aḥ* does. A vārttika makes it *ru* before *rūpa*, *rātri* and *rathantara*, and there *o* appears: <Sa iast="ahorātraḥ" />, "day and night". Before a case ending the rule just before it gives *ru* instead, and then *o* does appear: <Sa iast="ahobhyām" /> (<SutraRef id="8.2.68" />).

## r before r

<SutraFolio id="8.3.14" />

<SutraFolio id="6.3.111" />

An *r* before another *r* is dropped, and an *a*, *i* or *u* before it becomes long. Varadarāja: <Sa iast="punā ramate" />, "he enjoys again", <Sa iast="harī ramyaḥ" />, "Hari is delightful", <Sa iast="śambhū rājate" />, "Śambhu shines".

<Voice quote="kas-6.3.111" voice="bhasya">
  The rule covers two drops, of *ḍh* and of *r*. The Kāśikā's first examples are inside words, *līḍham* and *mūḍhaḥ*, where a *ḍh* has been dropped; then come the *r* examples, *nīraktam*, *agnī rathaḥ*, *indū rathaḥ*, *punā raktaṃ vāsaḥ* and *prātā rājakrayaḥ*.
</Voice>

## saḥ and eṣaḥ

<SutraFolio id="6.1.132" />

The nominatives <Sa iast="saḥ" />, "he, that", and <Sa iast="eṣaḥ" />, "this", lose their ending before a consonant: <Sa iast="eṣa viṣṇuḥ" />, "this is Viṣṇu", <Sa iast="sa śambhuḥ" />, "he is Śambhu". Not the forms with the syllable *ak* that the suffix *akac* inserts (<Sa iast="eṣakaḥ" />), not in a negative compound, and not before a vowel, where the ordinary rules apply: <Sa iast="so 'tra" />, "he is here".

<Voice quote="kas-6.1.132" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā tests each condition with a pair. Other pronouns keep the ending: *yo dadāti*, "who gives". So do the forms with *ak*: *eṣako dadāti*. So does the negative compound: *aneṣo dadāti*. And before a vowel: *eṣo 'tra*, *so 'tra*.
</Voice>

<SutraFolio id="6.1.134" />

In verse, *saḥ* may lose its ending before a vowel too, when that is needed to fill the metre: <Sa iast="saiṣa" />, from *sa* + *eṣa* by vṛddhi (<SutraRef id="6.1.88" />). The Kāśikā's main examples are Vedic; it reports that some take "line" to include the lines of ordinary verse, and quotes this one:

<Voice quote="kas-6.1.134" voice="bhasya">
  Each line opens with *saiṣa*, two syllables where *sa eṣa* would have three. The Kāśikā's counter-example shows the condition: in *sa iva vyāghro bhavet*, "he would be like a tiger", the metre does not need it, so 6.1.134 does not apply. The s still goes, by 8.3.17 and 8.3.19, but the vowels stay apart: *sa iva*, not *seva*.
</Voice>

<Voice quote="vp-1.87" voice="darsana">
  The *y* of *devāy iha*, the *ru* of *śivo 'rcyaḥ*: forms that the derivation passes through and the ear rarely or never hears. Bhartṛhari says something similar of the words we pick out inside a sentence: taking them to exist is the listener's limitation, and a means of grasping.
</Voice>
