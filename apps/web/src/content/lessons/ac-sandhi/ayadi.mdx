The ik vowels have semivowels of their own. The four diphthongs <Sa iast="e ai o au" /> (the pratyāhāra <Sa iast="ec" />, which the tradition calls <Sa iast="sandhyakṣara" />, "joined sounds") do not: each is made at two places at once, throat and palate (e, ai) or throat and lips (o, au), and no semivowel is made at the throat. Before a vowel they are handled by the next sūtra, which carries <Sa iast="aci" />, "when a vowel follows", over from 6.1.77.

<SutraFolio id="6.1.78" level={2} />

The rule has four substitutes, <Sa iast="ay av āy āv" />, written together as one compound. Each is a vowel of the a-class plus a semivowel, and each mirrors its diphthong. An *e* is in effect *a* + *i* pronounced as one sound; before a vowel it comes apart as *a* + *y*. *o* comes apart as *a* + *v*; the long-onset *ai* and *au* as *āy* and *āv*.

## Pairing in order

Which of the four replaces which? 1.1.50 could choose by similarity, but a simpler rule settles it first, and Varadarāja places it here.

<SutraFolio id="1.3.10" />

Two lists of equal length are paired member by member: <Sa iast="e" /> with <Sa iast="ay" />, <Sa iast="o" /> with <Sa iast="av" />, <Sa iast="ai" /> with <Sa iast="āy" />, <Sa iast="au" /> with <Sa iast="āv" />. The *ec* list runs e, o, ai, au because that is the order of the Śivasūtras: <Sa iast="eoṅ" />, <Sa iast="aiauc" />. Pāṇini wrote his substitutes in the same order.

Patañjali, you saw in the last lesson, used this rule to argue that 1.1.50 is not needed for 6.1.77 either: *ik* and *yaṇ* are lists of four, so they pair up in order as well.

<Voice quote="kas-6.1.78" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā's first four examples are inside words, where a stem meets a suffix: *ce* + *ana* gives *cayanam* "gathering", *lo* + *ana* gives *lavanam* "cutting", *cai* + *aka* gives *cāyakaḥ* "gatherer", *lau* + *aka* gives *lāvakaḥ* "reaper". The words *yathāsaṃkhyam*, "in order", come straight from 1.3.10.
</Voice>

Varadarāja's examples under 1.3.10 are of the same kind:

<div class="example">
  <span><Sa iast="hare" /> + <Sa iast="e" /> → <Sa iast="haraye" />, "for Hari": e becomes ay.</span>
  <span><Sa iast="viṣṇo" /> + <Sa iast="e" /> → <Sa iast="viṣṇave" />, "for Viṣṇu": o becomes av.</span>
  <span><Sa iast="nai" /> + <Sa iast="aka" /> → <Sa iast="nāyakaḥ" />, "leader": ai becomes āy.</span>
  <span><Sa iast="pau" /> + <Sa iast="aka" /> → <Sa iast="pāvakaḥ" />, "purifier, fire": au becomes āv.</span>
</div>

The stems *hare*, *viṣṇo*, *nai* and *pau* are not where these words start. *hari* and *viṣṇu* take guṇa before the dative ending *e*, and the roots *nī* "lead" and *pū* "purify" take vṛddhi before the suffix *aka*. Those changes are taught with declension and derivation. Here we pick up each word at its last step.

<Voice quote="vp-2.10" voice="darsana">
  *haraye* is analysed as stem *hare* plus ending *e*, and the *e* of the stem as *a* plus *y*. Bhartṛhari accepts such division as a method: stem and suffix are drawn out of a word just as words are drawn out of a sentence. The word for this, <Sa iast="apoddhāra" />, "lifting out", marks the parts as products of analysis.
</Voice>

## Before a suffix beginning with y

6.1.78 needs a following vowel. One more sūtra extends half of it to a consonant.

<SutraFolio id="6.1.79" />

<Sa iast="vāntaḥ" /> means "ending in v": of the four substitutes, only <Sa iast="av" /> and <Sa iast="āv" /> qualify, so the rule is about *o* and *au*. Before a **suffix** that begins with *y*, they open up just as before a vowel:

<div class="example">
  <span><Sa iast="go" /> + <Sa iast="ya" /> → <Sa iast="gavyam" />, "coming from cows".</span>
  <span><Sa iast="nau" /> + <Sa iast="ya" /> → <Sa iast="nāvyam" />, "navigable".</span>
  <span>A vārttika adds the measure of a road: <Sa iast="go" /> + <Sa iast="yūti" /> → <Sa iast="gavyūtiḥ" />, a measure of distance, though <Sa iast="yūti" /> is not a suffix.</span>
</div>

## Word-final e and o
