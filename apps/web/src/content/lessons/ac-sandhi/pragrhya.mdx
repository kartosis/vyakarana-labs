The last lesson ended with 6.1.125: a **pragṛhya** vowel stays as it is before a vowel. This lesson is about which vowels are pragṛhya, and about a second, optional way of leaving vowels unjoined.

## Duals in ī, ū and e

<SutraFolio id="1.1.11" level={2} />

A dual form that ends in *ī*, *ū* or *e* is <Sa iast="pragṛhya" />, "to be held apart". Varadarāja's examples, one for each vowel:

<div class="example">
  <span><Sa iast="harī etau" />, "these two Haris"</span>
  <span><Sa iast="viṣṇū imau" />, "these two Viṣṇus"</span>
  <span><Sa iast="gaṅge amū" />, "those two Ganges rivers"</span>
</div>

Without 1.1.11, *harī* + *etau* would become *haryetau* by 6.1.77, and *gaṅge* + *amū* would become *gaṅge 'mū* by 6.1.109. The pragṛhya vowel blocks every one of the rules you have learned.

<Voice quote="kas-6.1.125" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā's examples quote duals before the particle *iti*, the way they are cited in the padapāṭha of the Veda: *agnī iti*, *vāyū iti*, *khaṭve iti*, *māle iti*. Even before *iti*, which follows almost every quoted word, the dual vowel stays untouched.
</Voice>

## A pronoun and some particles

<SutraFolio id="1.1.12" />

After the *m* of the pronoun <Sa iast="adas" />, "that", *ī* and *ū* are pragṛhya: <Sa iast="amī īśāḥ" />, "those lords", and <Sa iast="rāmakṛṣṇāv amū āsāte" />, "those two, Rāma and Kṛṣṇa, are sitting". Varadarāja's counter-example: <Sa iast="amuke 'tra" />, where the *e* does not follow *m*, so 6.1.109 applies.

<SutraFolio id="1.1.14" />

A particle that is a single vowel is pragṛhya: <Sa iast="i indraḥ" />, <Sa iast="u umeśaḥ" />. The exception is <Sa iast="āṅ" />, the preposition *ā* that you met with 6.1.95. Varadarāja explains how to tell the two *ā*'s apart. Placed before a sentence, or expressing recollection, *ā* is not āṅ, and stays apart: <Sa iast="ā evaṃ nu manyase" />, "Ah, so that is what you think now!" In its other senses (slightly, up to) *ā* is āṅ and joins: <Sa iast="ā" /> + <Sa iast="uṣṇam" /> → <Sa iast="oṣṇam" />, "slightly warm".

<SutraFolio id="1.1.15" />

A particle ending in *o* is pragṛhya: <Sa iast="aho īśāḥ" />, "Oh, lords!"

<SutraFolio id="1.1.16" />

The *o* of a vocative (<Sa iast="viṣṇo" />, "O Viṣṇu") is not a particle, but before *iti* Śākalya allows it to be pragṛhya. Varadarāja gives all three results: <Sa iast="viṣṇo iti" />, <Sa iast="viṣṇa iti" /> (8.3.19) and <Sa iast="viṣṇaviti" /> (6.1.78).

<SutraFolio id="8.3.33" />

The particle <Sa iast="u" /> after a word ending in a consonant of the *may* class may become *v*: <Sa iast="kimv uktam" /> beside <Sa iast="kim u uktam" />, "what was said?".

## Śākalya's unjoined forms

<SutraFolio id="6.1.127" />

Here is the optional form that the tool on this site has been showing as "Also correct". In Śākalya's view, a word-final *i, u, ṛ* or *ḷ* before an **unlike** vowel may stay unjoined, and if it is long it becomes short. Varadarāja's example: <Sa iast="cakri atra" /> beside <Sa iast="cakryatra" />, from *cakrī* "the discus-bearer". He adds that because of the shortening there is no further vowel sandhi (<Sa iast="hrasvavidhisāmarthyān na svarasandhiḥ" />): if the short vowel went on to join, shortening it would have been pointless.

<Voice quote="kas-6.1.127" voice="bhasya">
  The Kāśikā's pairs give each unjoined form beside its joined one: *dadhi atra* and *dadhyatra*, *madhu atra* and *madhvatra*, *kumāri atra* and *kumāryatra*. The third shows the shortening: the stem is *kumārī*.
</Voice>

Two limits, both from Varadarāja. The ik must end a **word**: inside a word, *gaurī* + *au* gives only *gauryau*, "two fair women". There it may double its y by the next rule, <Sa iast="gauryyau" />:

<SutraFolio id="8.4.46" compact />

And this form of Śākalya's is not used **inside a compound**, by the vārttika on 6.1.127, <Sa iast="na samāse" />: *vāpī* + *aśvaḥ* gives only *vāpyaśvaḥ*.

<SutraFolio id="6.1.128" />

Before short *ṛ*, any word-final *ak* vowel, a or ā included, may stay unjoined and short in the same way: <Sa iast="brahma ṛṣiḥ" /> beside <Sa iast="brahmarṣiḥ" />. Here too the vowel must end a pada: inside a word, *āṛcchat* can only be *ārcchat*. But unlike 6.1.127, this option holds **even in a compound**, as the Siddhāntakaumudī notes: <Sa iast="saptaṛṣīṇām" /> beside <Sa iast="saptarṣīṇām" />.

<Voice quote="vp-2.38" voice="darsana">
  *dadhi atra* or *dadhyatra*, *hara iha* or *harayiha*, *go agram* or *go 'gram* or *gavāgram*: Pāṇini records the views of Śākalya, Sphoṭāyana and others as valid options. Bhartṛhari says why that is no weakness. The means by which grammar explains a word are taken up only to be let go, and no single method is binding. What is binding is the correct word.
</Voice>

## The whole prakaraṇa on one page

Then the options. At word end: drop y or v after a (<SutraRef id="8.3.19" />), or leave an ik (not in a compound) or an ak before ṛ unjoined (<SutraRef id="6.1.127" />, <SutraRef id="6.1.128" />). Anywhere: a consonant other than h may double after a vowel when no vowel follows it (<SutraRef id="8.4.47" />), and after a vowel followed by r or h (<SutraRef id="8.4.46" />). And for *go*, the rules of <SutraRef id="6.1.122" /> to <SutraRef id="6.1.124" />.
