## A consonant comes with a vowel

The basic unit of Devanagari is the <Sa iast="akṣara" />: a consonant, or a cluster of consonants, together with the vowel that follows. A bare consonant letter is read with a short *a*: <Sa slp="ka" /> is *ka*, not *k*.

To change the vowel, a **vowel sign** (<Sa iast="mātrā" />) is attached to the consonant. To remove the vowel altogether, a small stroke called the **virāma** goes underneath: <Sa slp="k" />.

Notice that the sign for short *i* is written **before** the consonant it follows in speech: <Sa slp="ki" />. You read it after the *k*.

## Clusters

When consonants meet with no vowel between them, Devanagari usually fuses them into a conjunct (<Sa iast="saṃyuktākṣara" />). A few conjuncts barely resemble their parts. Learn them as wholes:

## Signs on a syllable

- **Anusvāra** <Sa slp="aM" />: a dot above, a nasal after the vowel.
- **Candrabindu** <Sa slp="a~" />: a dot in a crescent, nasalising the vowel itself.
- **Visarga** <Sa slp="aH" />: two dots after the syllable, a breath.
- **Avagraha** <Sa slp="'" />: marks an *a* that has merged away in sandhi, as in <Sa iast="so 'yam" />.
- **Daṇḍa** <span lang="sa">।</span> and **double daṇḍa** <span lang="sa">॥</span>: the full stops of Sanskrit. Sūtras on this site end with a red <span lang="sa">॥</span>, as in a manuscript.

<Voice quote="vp-1.12" voice="darsana">
  Bhartṛhari speaks of speech "that has taken on distinct forms": divided into the sounds a script can record. Each akṣara on this page is one of those divisions. Grammar, he says, is the straight road from them to the essence of speech.
</Voice>
