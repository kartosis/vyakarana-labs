Pāṇini's grammar runs to about four thousand sūtras. Each is as short as it can be made, because the whole was meant to be memorised and recited. The price of that brevity is that a sūtra cannot be read like a sentence. It has to be unpacked, and the unpacking always follows the same four habits.

We will use one rule throughout, the first rule of vowel sandhi in the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī:

<SutraFolio id="6.1.77" level={2} />

<Voice quote="mbh-brhaspati" voice="bhasya">
  Patañjali's parable of why grammar needs rules: a word-by-word recitation never ends, even for the teacher of the gods. Rules that cover infinitely many words are the only finite way to know them. This is why every sūtra is general, and why it is so compressed.
</Voice>

## 1. Split the words

Sūtras are written in sandhi, so first undo it. This step is called <Sa iast="padaccheda" />, "cutting into words". <Sa iast="iko yaṇ aci" /> is three words: <Sa iast="ikaḥ" />, <Sa iast="yaṇ" />, <Sa iast="aci" />. Every folio on this site gives the word split first.

## 2. Read the cases

Pāṇini assigns a job to each grammatical case, and he states those jobs as rules of their own:

So <Sa iast="iko yaṇ aci" /> already reads: *in place of ik, yaṇ, when ac follows.*

## 3. Expand the names

<Sa iast="ik" />, <Sa iast="yaṇ" /> and <Sa iast="ac" /> are not words of the language. They are compressed names for sets of sounds, built from the Śivasūtras by a method you will learn in the next module:

- <Sa iast="ik" /> = i, u, ṛ, ḷ (with their long forms)
- <Sa iast="yaṇ" /> = y, v, r, l
- <Sa iast="ac" /> = every vowel

Now: *in place of i, u, ṛ or ḷ, a semivowel comes in when a vowel follows.*

## 4. Supply what is carried over

Some words a sūtra needs are not written in it at all. They continue in force from an earlier sūtra, which is called <Sa iast="anuvṛtti" />. Varadarāja states the habit outright in his gloss on the very first rule of the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī: <Sa iast="sūtreṣv adṛṣṭaṃ padaṃ sūtrāntarād anuvartanīyaṃ sarvatra" />, "a word not seen in a sūtra is to be carried over from another sūtra, everywhere."

Here the carried word is <Sa iast="saṃhitāyām" />, "in close junction", from <SutraRef id="6.1.72" />. That sūtra is a heading (<Sa iast="adhikāra" />) that governs a long stretch of rules after it. With it supplied, the rule is complete:

<p class="example"><span><strong>6.1.77, unpacked:</strong> when sounds run together, i, u, ṛ or ḷ is replaced by a semivowel if a vowel follows.</span></p>

## Which semivowel?

The rule offers four substitutes. The general principle <SutraRef id="1.1.50" />, <Sa iast="sthāne 'ntaratamaḥ" />, picks the one closest to the original. i is palatal, so it becomes the palatal y. u becomes v, ṛ becomes r, ḷ becomes l. Step through it:
